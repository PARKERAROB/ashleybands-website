## Carnegie Hall 2027: your questions

Both of Ashley's concert bands, the Concert Band and the Wind Ensemble, perform at Carnegie Hall in New York on Thursday, March 25, 2027. We travel March 23-26. These are the questions families ask most. Each answer reflects what we know as of October 27, 2026, and we'll update this page as answers come in.

### Why does this trip matter?

Most musicians never get to play on that stage. Our students earned it, and the plan is to take everyone who wants to go. Every gift lowers the cost for every student.

### How much will it cost my family?

Each student's share is **$2,000**. Gifts given for your student through their Music Notes link count toward it, and every general gift lowers the cost for every student. Anything credited above $2,000 goes to the whole trip fund. If you're worried about the cost, [talk to Mr. Parker](mailto:robert.parker@nhcs.net).

A family's payment toward a student's trip is not a charitable gift and is not tax-deductible.

### When are the payments and deadlines?

- **First payment: $650, our goal by Friday, October 30.** The $50 deposit counts toward it. The trip needs this money for our first group payment, but it is not required: no student loses their place for not paying by Friday. Pay what you can, when you can.
- **The rest:** pay any amount, any time, toward your student's $2,000 share. The final group payment to WorldStrides, our tour company, is due January 7, 2027.
- **Registration:** we start registering travelers with WorldStrides as payments start and students have funds in hand. WorldStrides' deadline is December 13. We'll tell you when it's your student's turn.
- **No refund protection.** Neither the group nor families have trip insurance through WorldStrides. Swapping a traveler's name after registration is allowed.
- **After you register,** WorldStrides' cancellation terms apply: $225 through November 22, then 25% of the trip price, 50% from January 7, and no refund from February 7.

### How do I pay?

Sign in to the [Family Portal](/portal) and choose **Payments**. You'll see your student's share, gifts given for them, what you've paid, and what's still owed.

- **Check or cash (best, every dollar counts).** Make checks payable to **Ashley Band Boosters**, with "Carnegie" and your student's name in the memo line, and turn it in at the band room. Then click **Record a check or cash I turned in**.
- **Online.** Click **Pay online**. PayPal keeps a small fee; you can add it so the full amount counts.

Music Notes is for supporters' gifts. Please make your family's own payments under Payments.

### Can I chaperone?

The trip has 10 official adult places: two directors and 8 chaperones, and only 3 chaperone places remain. Official chaperones pay **$2,500** per person in a double room, travel with the band, and are responsible for a group of 8 to 12 students for the whole trip. Asking to chaperone is a request, not a guarantee: Mr. Parker decides based on the trip's needs, including how many men and women are needed. Until he confirms you, plan to travel on your own (below).

To be confirmed, a chaperone pays in full, is approved as an NHCS Level 2 volunteer by December 10, and signs the [chaperone agreement](/info/carnegie-chaperone-agreement). Ask in the [Family Portal](/portal) under **Carnegie Hall 2027 → Adults coming to New York**.

### Can family come to New York?

Yes, two ways:

- **With the band, traveling on your own:** $2,500 per person in a double, or $2,000 per person in a quad when all four beds are filled (otherwise it becomes a double at $2,500). You get everything the band gets except travel to and from New York. In New York the group walks everywhere except the ferry to Ellis Island. You're welcome to help with students but don't have to.
- **Fully on your own:** no cost through the band. You plan and pay for everything, including your Carnegie Hall ticket. Just let us know you're coming.

Sign up in the [Family Portal](/portal) under **Carnegie Hall 2027 → Adults coming to New York**. Adults may pay in parts; please pay in full by **Monday, November 23**. Last call is the Winter Concert, Tuesday, December 8, and the final cutoff is **Thursday, December 10**. **Every adult payment is non-refundable.** You're added to the travel list only once you've paid in full.

### How do family tickets to the concert work?

Every traveler on the trip has a concert ticket. Family not on the trip buy tickets from the Carnegie Hall box office about six weeks before the concert, around mid-February. The festival guide lists them at $90.

### How does rooming work?

Students room 4 to a room, boys with boys and girls with girls. Adults room 2 to a room, or 4 to a quad when all four beds are filled. The directors are in single rooms.

1. **Lead the raffle or call businesses.** We need one parent to run the Winter Concert raffle, and more than 200 local businesses haven't been contacted yet. Reply to Mr. Parker's email or contact Mike Aycock at mdaycock@aol.com.

## Carnegie Hall 2027: Chaperone Agreement

**Ashley High School Bands · Carnegie Hall · March 23–26, 2027**

Thank you for offering to chaperone. On this trip, chaperones are part of the staff. Please read this carefully.

There are 10 official chaperone places, and most are already filled. Asking to chaperone is a request: Mr. Parker chooses official chaperones based on the trip's needs, including the number of men and women needed. Until Mr. Parker confirms you, plan to travel with the band on your own (getting yourself to and from New York).

Once Mr. Parker confirms you, your place is set when you have (1) paid in full, (2) been approved as an NHCS Level 2 volunteer, and (3) signed this agreement.

### 1. Before the trip

**NHCS Level 2 volunteer approval** (required by NHCS for every chaperone):

1. Review the NHCS volunteer orientation: https://new.express.adobe.com/webpage/CEj1D2XoaFiIZ
2. Complete the annual volunteer assessment with 80% or higher: https://docs.google.com/forms/d/e/1FAIpQLScVWKaIoXTNFKd1ofLKnSmK0P9Y73Xivcdb156cuEM0AGbnyg/viewform?usp=sf_link
3. Submit the Level 2 background check ($22.50, good for three years): https://securevolunteer.com/NHC/home

The background check must come back approved by **Thursday, December 10, 2026** or you can't travel as a chaperone. Start it now; it can take a few weeks. If the $22.50 fee is a hardship, contact Principal Perez (NHCS Policy 5015).

**Be part of the program before we go.** Come to the trip meetings (dates to come), know the itinerary, and get to know the students in your group.

**Share your contact information.** Your cell number goes to the directors, the other chaperones and the families in your group. You'll reach your students through the school-approved channel for the trip (planned: Microsoft Teams, pending Mr. Perez's approval). Under NHCS policy 4040/7310, chaperones count as staff and may not text, call, email or message a student one-to-one outside that channel, except in a real emergency.

**Tell the directors about your own health needs** that could affect the trip, privately.

### 2. Your students

Each chaperone is assigned a group of students, usually by hotel room: about two or three student rooms, 8 to 12 students.

For your group, you are responsible for:

- **Information.** Making sure they know the schedule, meeting times, what to wear and what to bring, every day.
- **Hotel.** Up and on time each morning; in their rooms and in bed at lights-out; room checks as the directors set them.
- **Every move.** Each time we move from one place to another (hotel, bus, meals, rehearsal, the performance, shows, sightseeing), your students are present, counted and ready to go. You report your count to a director before we leave.
- **First contact.** If one of your students needs something (medicine, a call home, feeling sick, a lost item, a problem with another student), you are the first person they come to. You handle it or bring it to a director. This keeps the directors free to run the trip, especially when the group splits up, such as for Broadway shows.
- **Medicine.** Chaperones do not give students any medicine, including over-the-counter items like Tylenol or allergy pills. Under NHCS policy 6125, only trained, authorized staff may give medicine, with the parent's and doctor's written forms. If a student needs medicine, you bring them to the staff member in charge of medicine. Students may carry only their own inhaler, EpiPen or diabetes supplies, when the school has approved it.
- **Families.** You may be the one contacting a family. Keep them informed through the directors' guidelines.

Group assignments may shift during the trip (for example, for Broadway shows). Be flexible.

### 3. On the trip, you are staff

- **There is no free time or personal time** during the trip. From departure to return, you are on duty.
- You follow the same expectations as an NHCS staff member: NHCS School Volunteers (policy 5015), Staff-Student Relations (4040/7310, which names volunteers as staff), School Safety (1510/4200/7270), employee dress and appearance standards (7340), and the Code of Student Conduct, which you help enforce. Be professional and dependable.
- Student information you're given (health, medicine, family contacts) is only for caring for your group on this trip. Keep it private and return or delete it when we're home.
- **No alcohol, tobacco, nicotine, vaping, cannabis or other drugs at any time during the trip,** including in your own room and during any down time. (Stricter than NHCS policies 5025 and 5026/7250, which already bar these around students and at school events.)
- No contact with students on personal social media.
- You stay with the group. No side trips, visitors or leaving the group without a director's OK.
- You're never alone in a room with a single student, and you don't enter student rooms except for room checks, and then with another adult present.
- No transporting students in a private vehicle or rideshare without a director's OK.
- If your own child is on the trip, you supervise your whole assigned group, not just your child, and your child follows the same rules as every student.
- Report any injury, illness, behavior problem or safety concern to a director right away.
- The directors' decisions on the trip are final. A chaperone who doesn't follow this agreement may be sent home at their own cost.

### 4. Cost

- **$2,500** per person, in a double room. This covers everything the band gets: travel to and from New York with the group, hotel, meals, the performance and the itinerary. In New York we get around on foot, except the ferry to Ellis Island.
- **You may pay in installments.** Please be paid in full by **Monday, November 23, 2026**. You're added to the WorldStrides traveler list only once you've paid in full.
- **Final deadline: Thursday, December 10, 2026.** No one can be added after that.
- **How to pay:** check or cash to Ashley Band Boosters is preferred. Online payment through ashleybands.com is fine; you can add the PayPal fee so your full amount counts.
- **Every payment is non-refundable.** There is no refund protection on this trip. If you can't go, talk with Mr. Parker; any money already paid stays with the trip until he has talked with you. Once you're registered with WorldStrides, its cancellation fees apply to your place.

### Signing

Once Mr. Parker confirms you as an official chaperone, he'll send you this agreement to sign. Questions? [Contact Mr. Parker](mailto:robert.parker@nhcs.net).

## Band Words, Explained

**MPA (Music Performance Adjudication):** a festival where each concert band performs for judges and earns a rating. Superior is the top rating. The NC Bandmasters Association runs it, and Ashley hosts one site each March.

**NCBA:** the North Carolina Bandmasters Association, the state band directors' group. "NCBA Eastern" is its eastern district.

**CCC (Conductor's Clinic and Concert):** a working week, then a weekend clinic and a concert. In 2026, guest clinicians worked with every Ashley band. This year's concert is February 27, 2027 at 3:00 PM.

**All-County, All-District and All-State:** honor bands made up of selected students from many schools. All-County is for New Hanover County schools. Students earn All-District and All-State spots by audition.

**Call time:** the time students must be in place and ready, in the band room or on campus as listed. It is earlier than the start time of the event.

**MEAC:** the Minnie Evans Arts Center at Ashley. The band room is in this building.

**WSW:** the Wilmington Symphonic Winds, a community wind band in Wilmington.

## Marching Band 2026

This page covers the 2026 marching band season: the schedule, what is expected, the cost, and how it is funded.

The Screaming Eagle Regiment is a competitive marching band. Sign-up for the 2026 season is closed.

## The Season at a Glance

The [band calendar](/calendar) has all marching band dates, times, and locations. Check it often. It is kept current as the season develops.

Regular rehearsals run Tuesdays and Thursdays, 4:00 - 7:00 PM, plus Saturdays on non-competition weeks. Colorguard and leadership have additional times. Every specific date lives on the calendar.

## What Is Expected

Marching band is a season-long commitment. Students are expected to:

- attend rehearsals consistently
- be present for summer preparation, band camp, and leadership/colorguard week
- attend all scheduled performances, including football games, competitions, NHCS district events, and parades
- keep up with music, drill, and equipment

## The Cost

The full competitive season costs roughly **$42,000 to $47,000** to operate. That covers staff, custom music, drill and choreography, colorguard uniforms and equipment, props, instruments and equipment, transportation, competition logistics, meals, water, and maintenance.

NHCS has covered transportation for the past two seasons, and we expect that to continue. If it does, the shared funding goal for the band is closer to **$35,000 to $41,000**.

With 60 students marching, that is roughly **$700 to $785 per student** in true cost, or about **$585 to $685 per student** as the shared goal.

No student pays a required individual fee. We ask each family to help meet a **$500 funding goal**. See [how we fund the season](/info/marching-band-funding) for the full breakdown and the ways to meet it.

## How It Is Funded

We set Fair-Share Funding Goals and ask every family to help meet them. Funding comes from:

- sponsorships (the largest source; one $2,000 sponsor can cover a student and several others)
- fundraisers
- booster support
- school and district support where available
- optional family contributions
- hardship support

No student is excluded from a band activity because of financial hardship. If cost is a concern, talk to Mr. Parker. That conversation is private.

## Competitive Marching Band Funding

This page shows what a competitive marching band season actually costs, and how we fund it together so no family carries it alone.

These are the real numbers. The season costs far more than any one family is asked to give. We set a shared funding goal, and we meet it together through sponsorships, fundraising, booster and district support, and optional family contributions. No student is ever kept off the field over money.

## What We Are Asking This Year

**60 students** are marching with the Screaming Eagle Regiment in 2026.

Our funding goal is **$500 per student**, which is **$30,000 total**.

- **This is a goal, not a bill.** No student pays a required fee to march.
- The $500 covers the costs due up front: custom music, drill, instructional staff, uniforms, and equipment.
- It does not cover the full cost of the season. The rest we raise together (see below).
- Families meet the $500 by paying, by bringing in sponsors, or both. We ask every family to help raise sponsorships.

## What a Competitive Season Actually Costs

A full competitive season costs roughly **$42,000 to $47,000** to operate.

NHCS has covered transportation for the past two seasons, and we expect that to continue. If it does, the shared funding goal for the band is closer to **$35,000 to $41,000**.

With 60 students marching, that works out to:

| | Total | Per student (60) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full true cost of the season | $42,000 - $47,000 | about $700 - $785 |
| Shared band goal (NHCS covers transport) | $35,000 - $41,000 | about $585 - $685 |
| **What we ask each family to help meet** | **$30,000** | **$500** |

The $500 ask is below the real per-student cost. We close the rest together.

## What the Cost Includes

A competitive marching band season is far more than performances at football games. A true season requires:

- custom music
- drill design
- body choreography
- instructional staff
- colorguard uniforms and equipment
- percussion support
- wind and percussion uniform tops
- UDB/BEAM drill and music app access
- props
- equipment, including instruments, wheels, sound, and front ensemble needs
- transportation
- competition logistics
- meals, water, maintenance, and replacement items

## How This Compares

Public information from other band programs shows a wide range of family contribution structures:

- North Carolina public examples ranged from about **$350 to $1,450** per student
- the broader multi-state sample ranged from about **$80 to $1,600** per student
- local and state competitive programs without major overnights often landed around **$350 to $600**
- higher-travel, staff-heavy, or national-level programs often landed around **$900 to $1,600+**

The biggest cost drivers are staff, custom music, drill, choreography, transportation, props, equipment, meals, and production, not contest entry fees.

## How We Fund It

We set Fair-Share Funding Goals and ask every family to help meet them. Funding comes from:

- **sponsorships** (the largest source; one $2,000 sponsor can cover a student and several others)
- fundraisers
- booster support
- school and district support where available
- optional family contributions
- hardship support

No student is excluded from a band activity because of financial hardship. If cost is a concern, talk with Mr. Parker. That conversation is private.

## Meeting Your $500

Sign-up is closed. If your student has not reached $500 yet, there are a few ways to close the gap:

- **Pay directly.** Pay online in the family portal at ashleybands.com/portal under Funding and payments, or drop a check or cash in the payment box in the band room.
- **Bring in a sponsor.** A local business or family friend can sponsor at ashleybands.com/sponsors, and the sponsorship is credited to your student. One $2,000 sponsor can cover your student and several others.
- **Donations count too.** Gifts from friends and family can be credited to your student.

The family portal shows what has been recorded for your student. If something you turned in is not showing, tell Mr. Parker.

On top of that, **we ask every family to pursue sponsorships regardless** of how they meet their $500. If every family reaches 5 businesses, that is about 300 local connections, every one tied personally to a student. The more sponsors we have, the less any family pays.

Students receive invitations to their class-specific chats through their school-managed NHCS student accounts. Google Chat functions like a secure school messaging system, and all messages are archived and viewable by NHCS personnel.

## Required Items

### Instrument

- Personal instruments: case labeled with student name and instrument serial number
- Rental instruments: available through Music & Arts, 2340 S. 17th Street, Wilmington, NC, (910) 791-4799
- School-owned instruments: available as inventory allows
- Marching band instruments: available as inventory allows
- Percussion: snare sticks, practice pad and stand, black hand towel

### Clothing

- Concert attire: [Musician Black details](/info/2026-2027-band-information)

- Pencil pouch
