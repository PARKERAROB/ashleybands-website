## Core Takeaway

The strongest through-line in the ensemble-pedagogy materials is that fundamentals are not a warmup block. They are the curriculum.

The useful rehearsal question is:

> Does the music sound like the fundamentals, or do fundamentals disappear once literature starts?

For Ashley, this page should inform both concert-band rehearsal design and the “music-first marching band” work. The same non-negotiables transfer:

- tone
- air
- response
- note shape
- balance
- intonation
- rhythm
- style
- listening
- independence

## Ashley Rehearsal Design

### 1. Fundamentals Must Transfer

The repeated warning across the fundamentals documents is that warmups become empty if they do not change the literature.

Ashley test:

- Did today’s fundamental block name a specific sound/skill?
- Did that same skill appear in the music within five minutes?
- Did students hear the connection?
- Did the ensemble improve the literature because of the fundamental, not merely before it?

### 2. Keep The Fundamental Vocabulary Small

A compact shared vocabulary is more useful than a large rotating menu.

Core Ashley vocabulary:

- air shape
- instant response
- note length
- release
- tonal center
- priority voice
- balance down
- match across
- subdivision
- style family
- phrase direction

This should align with marching-band musical non-negotiables in [tba-marching-band-season-planning.md](tba-marching-band-season-planning.md).

### 3. A Daily Drill Should Build Independence

The daily-drill materials push students toward awareness, not director dependence.

Useful drill sequence:

- Breathing/posture/air flow.
- Response and release.
- Unison tone and pitch.
- Articulation/note length.
- Scale or key work tied to literature.
- Chord/balance work.
- Short transfer excerpt from the day’s music.

The last step matters. Without transfer, it is just exercise.

### 4. Balance Comes Before Many Tuning Fixes

The balance and intonation sources repeatedly imply that many “tuning” problems are actually balance, tone, or voicing problems.

Useful student questions:

- Who has the melody?
- Who has the bass line?
- Who has the moving line?
- Who has color/support?
- Is my part primary, secondary, or background?
- Am I matching across or balancing down?

Ashley application:

- Mark priority voices in rehearsal plans.
- Use small chunks where students identify function before playing.
- Teach chord responsibility: root/fifth/third/seventh awareness as appropriate.

### 5. Style Needs A System

The style and march materials point toward explicit note-length systems, especially for marches and traditional literature.

Ashley application:

- Create a small style chart for each concert cycle.
- Reduce style language to a few repeatable families: connected, separated, lifted, accented, sustained.
- Teach the difference between melodic accents and rhythmic accents.
- Treat style as ensemble agreement, not individual interpretation.

### 6. Sight-Reading Is A Year-Round Literacy Routine

The sight-reading documents converge on routine:

- keys and scales
- rhythm vocabulary
- counting system
- accidentals
- rests and entrances
- repeats/endings
- student responsibility during explanation
- consistent checklist order

Ashley application:

- Do not treat sight-reading as only a contest room skill.
- Build a weekly literacy block into concert season.
- Use a standard student checklist: key, meter, tempo, repeats, accidentals, rhythms, rests, dynamics, roadmap, style.
- Have students physically touch or mark risk points during silent study when appropriate.

### 7. Rehearsal Plans Should Name The Point

The rehearsal-planning sources argue that written plans prevent drift.

Useful rehearsal-plan fields:

- today’s sound priority
- literature target
- transfer fundamental
- rhythm/literacy target
- balance/style target
- assessment/listening moment
- what gets cut if time runs short

This is especially useful for concert-prep weeks and sub-varsity ensembles.

### 8. Non-Varsity Bands Need Serious Musical Goals

The second/third band materials repeatedly warn against treating lower ensembles as less important.

Ashley application:

- Give Concert Band a real sound goal, not only “get through the notes.”
- Build buy-in through achievable musical success.
- Choose repertoire that lets the group sound good and grow.
- Keep individual accountability humane but real.
- Avoid giving the best teaching energy only to the top ensemble.

## Concert Cycle Checklist

Before choosing or rehearsing a concert cycle:

- What sound should this ensemble have by performance week?
- What fundamentals will build that sound?
- What keys/rhythms/styles does the music require?
- What daily drill transfers directly into the pieces?
- What are the three highest-value cleaning spots?
- What musical responsibilities can students identify without prompting?
- What will the non-varsity group do successfully and proudly?
- What will be assessed or recorded each week?

## Marching Carryover

The ensemble materials reinforce the same idea as the marching-band season notes:

- add movement only after students know the musical goal
- do not let feet/body erase tone, note length, or style
- use marching season to strengthen full-year fundamentals
- build a common vocabulary that survives across marching and concert seasons
