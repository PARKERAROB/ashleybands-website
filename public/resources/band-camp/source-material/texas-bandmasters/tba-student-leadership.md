## Core Takeaway

The TBA leadership materials converge on one point: student leadership is a training system, not a title system.

Good leadership work has four parts:

- Select students for trust, service, and teachability, not only talent or popularity.
- Define real jobs and expectations before students need to act.
- Train communication, accountability, conflict handling, and peer teaching.
- Follow up throughout the year so leadership grows after camp.

For Ashley, this is directly relevant to July 27-30 leadership week / preseason and to how section leaders support the first month of SER.

## Selection Principles

Useful criteria:

- trustworthy
- service-oriented
- consistent under stress
- able to model the desired behavior
- respected by peers without needing to dominate
- willing to learn how to communicate
- able to create success for others

Avoid:

- popularity-only selection
- chair-order-only selection
- creating positions just to give titles
- assuming a strong player is automatically a strong peer leader
- giving responsibility without adult coaching

## Ashley Selection Filter

Before naming or confirming roles, ask:

- What exact job does this student need to do?
- Does this role solve a real program need?
- What adult support will the student get?
- What does success look like by the end of week one?
- What should this student never be asked to handle alone?

## Training Targets

### Communication

Students need direct teaching on:

- how to approach peers respectfully
- how to give specific feedback
- how to ask a question instead of issuing a command
- how to redirect without embarrassment
- when to bring a problem to an adult

Ashley application: rehearse the actual phrases leaders should use during basics, sectionals, and breaks.

### Accountability

Peer accountability is usually where student leaders struggle. They either avoid the issue or overcorrect.

Train:

- private before public
- specific before general
- behavior before character
- repair before punishment
- adult escalation for safety, repeated conflict, or sensitive issues

### Service

Leadership should be framed as helping the group function, not gaining authority.

Possible SER jobs:

- new-member check-ins
- section attendance/whereabouts awareness
- equipment readiness
- basics block setup
- music pass-off support
- uniform/help desk support
- section morale scan
- “who needs help before rehearsal?” check

### Peer Teaching

Peer teaching should be structured enough to protect both students:

- assign a narrow skill
- give a model
- provide a time limit
- debrief what worked
- do not let student leaders become substitute directors

## Leadership Week Skeleton

### Day 1: Identity And Service

- What SER needs from leaders this year.
- Role descriptions.
- Service vs. authority.
- “What problem does your role solve?”

### Day 2: Communication

- Giving feedback.
- Asking better questions.
- Redirecting peers.
- Practicing exact language for basics/rehearsal.

### Day 3: Systems

- Attendance/check-in flow.
- Section responsibilities.
- Equipment/uniform support.
- New-member check-ins.
- How leaders report issues to staff.

### Day 4: Application

- Leaders teach a short skill.
- Leaders run a small check-in.
- Staff gives feedback.
- Set first-week goals by section.

## In-Season Cadence

The recurring message in the TBA sources is follow-up.

Suggested Ashley cadence:

- Weekly 10-minute leader huddle during summer band.
- One written section snapshot each week: attendance, morale, music concern, marching concern, one student who needs support.
- Early September reset: what is working, what needs adult help, what should stop.
- Mid-season fatigue check before the October load.
- Post-season reflection before leadership fades into memory.

## Concert Ensemble Carryover

One source focuses on student leadership in concert ensembles. The useful BDOS connection is that leadership should not disappear after marching season.

Concert-season roles could include:

- chamber group captain
- setup/reset lead
- tuning-process model
- section music checklist
- peer practice partner
- recording/reflection captain

This keeps leadership tied to musicianship instead of only marching logistics.

## Boundaries

Student leaders should not handle:

- discipline consequences
- private student/family information
- money
- parent communication
- bullying/harassment concerns without adult involvement
- medical or safety decisions
- emotionally loaded peer conflict without adult support
