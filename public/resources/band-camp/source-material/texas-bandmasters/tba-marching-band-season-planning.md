## Core Takeaway

The strongest pattern across the TBA marching-band materials is that marching season works best when treated as a single designed system:

- Spring: decide the show, communication plan, staff roles, registration, supplies, and student-leader structure.
- Summer: teach simple repeatable movement/music systems, build social trust, and lock core procedures.
- Fall: protect academics, communicate relentlessly, pace fatigue, and clean by priority.
- Posture: musical quality stays the non-negotiable even when feet, body, staging, electronics, and production demands are added.

## Ashley Applications

### 1. Build the 2026 SER Season Control Sheet

Create one current sheet or page for the season that includes:

- Show concept and intent.
- Music-first non-negotiables.
- Big effect moments.
- Current design-team dependencies.
- Summer band objectives by week.
- Student-leader responsibilities.
- Parent communication schedule.
- Supplies and registration status.

This belongs in `projects/marching-band/`, not in `now.md`.

### 2. Name The Musical Non-Negotiables

The music-first handout frames marching fundamentals as an extension of the full-year musicianship curriculum. For Ashley, the useful move is to name a short set of SER musical non-negotiables before summer band:

- characteristic tone while moving
- consistent note lengths/style vocabulary
- ensemble starts, changes, and releases
- dynamic shape, not just volume
- balance to priority voices
- student feedback loop during fundamentals

Then every marching warmup should expose and fix one of those items.

### 3. Separate Design Problems From Rehearsal Problems

The design-team communication handout is mostly a warning against letting missing or late design information turn into student stress. Useful rule for Ashley:

- If a problem requires the designer, solve it with the designer.
- If students can improve the current version, keep rehearsing the cleanable layer.
- Do not let unknown future rewrites paralyze today’s rep.

For BDOS, that means maintaining a short design-dependency list during June/July:

- waiting on music change
- waiting on drill/staging
- waiting on guard/equipment choice
- waiting on prop/electronics/logistics
- student-facing action while waiting

### 4. Make Summer Band Simple On Purpose

The summer-band notes repeatedly push simplicity:

- movement is walking with control
- only teach the marching skills the show actually needs
- keep music fundamentals simple and directly transferable
- run fundamentals at show tempos when possible
- build social/culture time into the schedule intentionally

Ashley implication: summer band plans should not become a clinic on every possible marching technique. They should teach the few techniques this show needs, then apply them quickly to music and drill.

### 5. Clean By Priority

The cleaning-process notes point toward triage:

- Pick the musical impact moments.
- Decide what must be loud and what must not.
- Clean from actual drill/staging situations.
- Rewrite/revoice if the payoff is not worth the work.
- Mark style clearly and reduce unnecessary lines.
- Treat choreography as something that must be cleanable, not just impressive.

Ashley implication: by early September, each movement needs a short “cleaning priority” list:

- top musical arrival
- top visual arrival
- weakest transition
- highest-risk timing area
- student-leader support role

### 6. Treat Leadership As Training, Not Selection

The student-leadership notes align on this point: picking good students is not enough. Leadership needs a repeatable training cadence.

Useful Ashley structure:

- Before summer: clarify roles and expectations.
- During summer band: teach communication, service, accountability, and problem-solving.
- During season: hold short regular leadership meetings tied to the week’s goals.
- Mid-season: use leaders to surface fatigue, morale, and section-level problems.
- Spring: invite potential future leaders into low-stakes training before formal selection.

### 7. Recruiting/Retention Is All Year

The recruiting handouts frame recruitment as repeated trust-building, not one event.

Potential Ashley sequence:

- Fall: middle-school visibility, football-game experience, first incoming-family message.
- January-February: next-year calendar and “what high school band is like” messaging.
- March-April: workshops, show clues, guard/drumline/movement on-ramps.
- May: Signing Day and detailed summer-band reminders.
- Summer: social touch before full summer band.

This connects directly to the current Lane 1/2/3 onboarding work.

## Reusable Checklist

- Is the show built around student success, not adult cleverness?
- Are transitions designed, not merely connected?
- Are the hardest music/visual demands educationally worth the rehearsal cost?
- Does each staff member know the same language, priorities, and protocols?
- Are parents getting predictable information before anxiety creates extra email?
- Are student leaders being trained weekly, or only named once?
- Are we protecting academics and fatigue before they become fall crises?
