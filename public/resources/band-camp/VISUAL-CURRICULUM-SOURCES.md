components and the place to begin designing band camp's outdoor visual blocks.

task is to translate their teaching progression into independent student capabilities, ownership
evidence, and appropriately sequenced rehearsal blocks.

## Confirmed instructional ideas

The readable source text establishes several planning principles:

- Use a written visual-fundamentals progression across all instructional staff.
- Do not skip prerequisite steps.
- Expect to revisit concepts rather than assuming one exposure creates learning.
- Intentionally alternate detail-focused and repetition-focused rehearsal.
- Ensure instructors understand why each technique is taught.
- Set goals for the day, week, and longer arc before selecting exercises.
- Use concise, specific feedback.
- Keep expectations consistent and the environment predictable.
- Use student modeling when a student demonstrates mastery.
- Maintain enough drill variety to transfer skills without creating confusion.
- Build a consistent, comprehensive routine that students can eventually own.
- Teach the whole student, including physical and emotional needs.

## First translation pass

Before building daily visual plans, define these for every module:

| Required definition | Planning question |
|---|---|
| Independent capability | What can the student do without director operation? |
| Prerequisites | What must already be understood or executable? |
| Standard | What exact body, timing, spatial, or equipment conditions define success? |
| Ownership evidence | What can staff observe that proves students own it? |
| Transfer task | Where does the technique appear in unfamiliar drill or show content? |
| Stress test | Can students retain it under fatigue, heat, tempo, direction changes, or divided attention? |
| Recovery path | What happens when the evidence shows the capability is not yet owned? |

Only after this translation should the modules be assigned to weeks, days, and the 7:00-10:00
outdoor blocks.
