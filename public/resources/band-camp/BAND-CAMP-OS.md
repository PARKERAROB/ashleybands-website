# Ashley Band Camp OS

_Working draft 0.1 — component discovery_

## Purpose

Band camp installs, validates, and stress-tests the operating system of the ensemble before
rehearsal time becomes scarce during the school year.

By the beginning of school, routine rehearsal should no longer require the director to constantly
operate the system.

## Design standard

For every component, answer:

1. What is it?
2. Why does it exist?
3. What observable evidence shows students own it by the end of camp?

The evidence must describe something students can do reliably, including under fatigue, heat,
complexity, and repetition. “Students were told” and “we covered it” are not evidence of ownership.

## Constitutional assumptions

- Students want to be here.
- The system is invitation-based rather than coercive.
- Choice precedes commitment.
- Commitment precedes accountability.
- Trust is earned as an outcome of shared desire, commitment, contribution, and improvement.

## Organizational principles

### Commit

Choose intentionally, honor commitments, and renegotiate honestly when circumstances change.

### Contribute

Leave the ensemble stronger than you found it.

### Improve

Continue thinking, refining, and growing.

## Ownership progression

> Choose -> Commit -> Learn -> Try -> Do -> Own

Teacher progression:

> Teach -> Guide -> Assess -> Release

Student progression:

> Learn -> Try -> Do -> Own

Release is earned through observable performance, not assumed because instruction occurred.

## Operating-system component inventory

This is a first component map derived from the conversation. The boundaries and names remain open
for refinement.

| Component | What it is | Why it exists | End-of-camp evidence of student ownership |
|---|---|---|---|
| Call time and readiness | The shared definition of when a member is present, equipped, prepared, and ready to begin. | Protects rehearsal time and turns punctuality into collective readiness rather than physical arrival. | Students arrive and become fully ready by call time without repeated director prompting; missing people or materials are surfaced through the agreed system. |
| Rehearsal start | The repeatable sequence that moves the ensemble from readiness into focused work at the block start time. | Creates a dependable beginning and prevents attention, setup, or leadership delays from consuming the block. | The ensemble begins the assigned mode of work on time from the established cue, with student leaders handling routine setup and alignment. |
| Transitions | Explicit blocks and procedures for moving people, equipment, attention, and information between activities or locations. | Makes the true cost of movement visible and keeps rehearsal blocks honest. | The ensemble completes transitions safely within their allocated time and arrives ready for the next block without the director individually routing people. |
| Dismissal and reset | The process for closing a block or day, accounting for people and equipment, communicating what comes next, and leaving spaces ready. | Protects equipment, facilities, information, and the next rehearsal. | Students complete the reset and leave only after the defined conditions are met; the next call and individual responsibilities are understood. |
| Communication | The agreed channels, timing, message ownership, acknowledgement, and escalation path for information. | Ensures the right people receive and act on information without the director becoming the sole router. | Students and leaders use the correct channel, acknowledge actionable information, ask clarifying questions, and escalate exceptions through the agreed path. |
| Equipment stewardship | The system for issuing, transporting, staging, using, checking, and returning instruments, props, uniforms, and rehearsal equipment. | Protects shared resources and prevents equipment confusion from interrupting learning. | Equipment moves through rehearsal safely and predictably; students identify and correct missing, misplaced, unsafe, or damaged items through the system. |
| Leadership | The defined responsibilities, authority, boundaries, and service expectations of student leaders. | Distributes routine operation while developing judgment rather than creating substitute directors. | Leaders initiate assigned systems, make decisions within their authority, help others succeed, and escalate matters outside their role without waiting for director rescue. |
| Governance and decisions | How routine decisions are made, who owns them, how input is gathered, and how commitments can be honestly renegotiated. | Makes authority legible and connects voluntary choice to real accountability. | Students can identify who decides, contribute through the proper route, carry out settled decisions, and surface a needed renegotiation rather than silently disengaging. |
| Rehearsal response system | The shared way students receive instruction, attempt work, use feedback, reset, repeat, and demonstrate improvement. | Turns rehearsal from director-controlled repetition into a learning system students can operate. | Students respond to cues, make purposeful attempts, apply feedback, self-correct known issues, and sustain productive repetitions with decreasing intervention. |
| Expectations and accountability | A small set of observable commitments and the fair process for addressing when reality differs from them. | Converts values into usable choices and protects the ensemble without relying on coercion or surprise. | Students can explain the commitments, meet them routinely, acknowledge misses, repair effects, and renegotiate honestly when circumstances change. |
| Contribution and ensemble care | The expectation that each person notices and acts on ways to leave people, spaces, resources, and the ensemble stronger. | Makes teamwork, service, and shared responsibility concrete. | Students help solve visible needs without waiting for individualized orders and do so without abandoning their own responsibilities. |
| Improvement loop | The process for noticing results, reflecting, choosing a refinement, testing it, and retaining what works. | Keeps the operating system adaptive and teaches students to think rather than merely comply. | Students and leaders can identify a gap, propose or apply a change, evaluate its effect, and incorporate the improvement into the next repetition. |

## Typical day — working schedule

This is the current daily frame. It is a planning constraint, not yet a detailed rehearsal plan.
The contents of each rehearsal block will be derived from outcomes after the operating-system
components are approved.

| Time | Block | Operational meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 7:00–10:00 | Outdoor visual rehearsal | Students are ready to begin outdoor work at 7:00. |
| 10:00–10:15 | Transition indoors | Movement, equipment, hydration, and readiness for music are visible scheduled work. |
| 10:15–11:45 | Music rehearsal | Indoor musical capability and application work. |
| 11:45–12:30 | Lunch, including transition | Lunch and all movement into and out of lunch fit inside the stated block. |
| 12:30–1:50 | Discipline-specific work | Separate work by relevant discipline; exact groupings and outcomes remain to be defined. |
| 1:50–2:00 | Transition to full band | All groups move and become ready for combined band. |
| 2:00–2:50 | Combined band | Full-ensemble capability and application work. |
| 2:50–3:00 | Cleanup, announcements, dismissal | The final operational block: reset people, equipment, spaces, and information. |
| 3:00 | Camp ends; students released | The end time is firm. |

### Schedule rules

- Block start times are firm.
- Students know the readiness condition required at each start time.
- Transition time is explicit and may not be silently borrowed by the preceding rehearsal.
- The final ten minutes are an instructional and operational block, not leftover time.
- Cleanup, reset, announcements, and dismissal must be completed within the final block.
- Students are released and camp ends at 3:00.

## Visual capability engine

The 7:00-10:00 outdoor visual blocks should begin from Rob's **Dynamic Marching & Movement - The
System** materials rather than from a newly invented visual curriculum.

The source progression currently suggests this capability sequence:

1. Posture and the fundamentals-block grid
2. Instrument carriage
3. Mark time and timing awareness
4. Forward marching
5. Backward marching
6. Slides
7. Effort changes
8. Body movement
9. Stretch and movement routine
10. Pre-show routine
11. Fitness and physical preparation

This is a source sequence, not yet the camp sequence. Before assigning it to days or activities,
each area must be translated into:

- an end-of-camp independent capability;
- prerequisite capabilities;
- observable ownership evidence;
- instruction, guided practice, assessment, and release;
- and one or more stress-test conditions.

The Dynamic Marching teaching guidance also supports the operating-system model: use a written
progression consistently across staff, do not skip prerequisite steps, revisit concepts, distinguish
detail-focused work from repetition-focused work, teach the reason behind technique, set explicit
goals, use concise feedback, model exemplars, and maintain a predictable learning environment.

See `VISUAL-CURRICULUM-SOURCES.md` for the Drive folders and module inventory.

## Physical preparation source

The starting source for strength and conditioning is Forte Athletics' **Marching Strong Rehearsal
Workout System 2026**. The recovered free sample contains one complete Phase 1 strength workout,
leader instructions, safety guidance, movement modifications, an exercise library, and linked
training videos. It is the first of nine workouts in Forte's full twelve-week system.

The source suggests a short opening routine three days per week, but that is not yet Ashley's
schedule. Before assigning it to a block, the camp plan must define the independent physical
capability being developed, the qualified adult responsible, the student-leader training and
release standard, applicable school safety procedures, and the evidence used to determine whether
students can perform the movements safely and independently.

See `PHYSICAL-CONDITIONING-SOURCES.md` for the archived PDF, source links, workout inventory, and
governance questions.

## Drill-learning operations source

Ashley uses Ultimate Drill Book Pro, and Rob's working position is **dot over form, with both
required**. The starting operational source is the 2023 Texas Bandmasters/UDB handout describing the
AC/DC process:

- Animation;
- Cross Count; and
- Direction Change.

The broader research collection covers coordinate and field literacy, set-by-set and batch
learning, pathway and step-size responsibility, check/adjust/reset procedures, linked repetitions,
music integration, cleaning lenses, and student ownership. These are research inputs rather than a
finished Ashley procedure.

See `DRILL-LEARNING-RESEARCH-COLLECTION.md` and the archived source under
`source-material/drill-learning/`.

## Questions the component pass must resolve

- Are safety, health, hydration, and heat response their own operating-system component or a
  non-negotiable standard embedded across every component?
- Is attendance/absence handling part of call time, communication, governance, or a separate
  component?
- Does culture require its own component, or is it the accumulated result of Commit, Contribute,
  Improve, and the operating system in use?
- Which components are owned by all students, which by student leaders, which by staff, and which
  are shared?
- What specific failures should the camp stress tests deliberately expose?
- What minimum standard counts as “owned,” and what happens when evidence shows a component is not
  yet owned?

## Planning sequence after component definition

1. Approve the component set and ownership evidence.
2. Define end-of-camp outcomes across the operating system, musical engines, and visual engines.
3. Sequence those outcomes by week.
4. Derive daily outcomes.
5. Derive block outcomes.
6. Choose activities and year-specific applications that produce and test those outcomes.
7. Add explicit transition blocks.
8. Run a coherence check: every block supports an outcome, and every required outcome has enough
   instruction, guided practice, assessment, release, and stress testing.
