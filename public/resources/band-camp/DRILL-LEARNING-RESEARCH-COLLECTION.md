## AC/DC source recovered

The verified source is:

**How to Change the Drill Learning Process for You and Your Students Forever, No Matter What Your
Band Size Is**, Reid Atkinson, James Cordova Jr., Calvin Edwards, Horacio Gomez, and Emily Petty,
Texas Bandmasters Association Convention/Clinic, 2023.

- [Official TBA source](https://apps.texasbandmasters.org/archives/pdfs/clinic/2023_atkinson.pdf)
- [Archived project copy](source-material/drill-learning/2023-Atkinson-ACDC-Drill-Learning.pdf)

The handout directly incorporates Ultimate Drill Book and carries the UDB mark. It is a TBA clinic
handout about a UDB-enabled teaching process, which explains why it was remembered as both an
Ultimate Drill Book resource and part of the Texas educator corpus.

### Exact AC/DC process

After setting a dot, students engage with three pieces of transition information:

1. **Animation** — watch the animation to check the pathway.
2. **Cross Count** — identify and point to the cross count to begin establishing the correct
   movement pattern.
3. **Direction Change** — practice the previous step into the set (`-1`) and the first step in the
   next direction (`+1`).

The handout says AC/DC can be used regardless of the broader drill-learning system. Its purpose is
to make students engage with critical movement information immediately rather than treating drill
as a list of disconnected destinations.

## Complete process surrounding AC/DC

### Before rehearsal

Students can study drill in UDB before reaching the field. The handout describes students viewing
drill count by count, connecting drill with music, and sometimes arriving able to work
independently. This makes pre-rehearsal preparation a possible capability to teach and assess, not
an assumption.

### Two acquisition models

**Set by set**

- Learn one set.
- Staff verifies placement.
- Move to the next set.
- The ensemble processes the same layer at roughly the same time.

**Batch learning**

- Students receive time to learn approximately four to six sets.
- Staff seek out and assist students who need support.
- More advanced students continue into pathway, cross-count, and step-size information.
- Later instruction can focus on execution rather than repeatedly supplying destinations.

The source does not require every ensemble to use batch learning. It presents active student
engagement as the larger goal.

### Six-stage batch-learning progression

1. **Self-guided learning** — students learn a phrase of coordinates, then examine midsets and cross
   counts as ready.
2. **Run to sets and verbalize** — students move directly to each destination and state their
   coordinate using consistent field language.
3. **Turn drill into fundamentals** — add technique and equal step size; work individual
   transitions, overlapping pairs, and progressively longer chunks.
4. **Run continuously** — connect the phrase while emphasizing direction changes, count structure,
   pathways, and later moving air.
5. **Add music** — establish music in place, then combine it with motion while visual and music
   staff retain distinct feedback priorities.
6. **Clean drill** — continue refining midsets, cross counts, pathways, and other transition
   information across the season.

The handout also describes UDB rehearsal mode, reduced-tempo metronome work, and “prep drill,” in
which students vocalize the final count of one set and first count of the next to focus on pulse,
direction change, and step-size adjustment.

## Dot, form, and the useful hybrid

### Coordinate-first evidence

[Frank Troyka's Texas Bandmasters clinic
handout](https://apps.texasbandmasters.org/archives/pdfs/clinic/2003-troyka1.pdf) recommends heavy
coordinate reliance early, followed by increasing attention to form and interval for performance.

[Mitch Rogers' coordinate-system
article](https://apps.texasbandmasters.org/archives/pdfs/bmr/2012-06-rogers.pdf) frames coordinates
as a means of rehearsal efficiency, individual control, and performer accountability.

[Kennesaw State's marching
handbook](https://www.kennesaw.edu/marching-owls/athletic-bands/marching-handbook.php) separates
three necessary references:

- field/coordinate reference for endpoints and landmarks;
- step-size reference for distributing movement through the available counts; and
- form/body reference for orientation and relationship to nearby performers.

### Contrasting form-first evidence

[Murphy High School's technique
manual](https://www.murphyhsband.com/uploads/1/2/5/6/125687750/murphy_marching_band_techniques.pdf)
explicitly prioritizes form over the individual dot and gives different dressing procedures for
blocks, lines, arcs, circles, and diagonals. It is useful as a contrasting model and as evidence that
“dress the form” requires form-specific rules.

### Working research synthesis

One way to express Rob's position operationally, without yet adopting it as policy:

1. **Dot:** the endpoint is each performer's primary independent responsibility.
2. **Path:** pathway, cross counts, midsets, and step size determine how the performer gets there.
3. **Form:** interval, cover, alignment, curvature, and orientation identify ensemble-level errors
   and refine the visible result.
4. **Adjustment:** any move away from the written coordinate is deliberate, communicated, and
   traceable rather than an attempt to follow an inaccurate neighbor.

The performer is therefore responsible for both an absolute location and a relational role.

## Drill-information inventory

For each transition, a student may ultimately need to know:

- performer label;
- starting and ending coordinates;
- counts or measures;
- path type: direct, curved, rotation, follow the leader, reshape, or staged movement;
- step size;
- midset or cross-count checkpoints;
- relevant yard-line or hash crossings and their counts;
- travel direction;
- upper-body and instrument orientation;
- final step into the set;
- first step out;
- form and interval responsibility;
- collision or pass-through relationships; and
- relationship to music and pulse.

[Ultimate Drill Book's performer
view](https://help.ultimatedrillbook.com/kb/guide/en/selecting-a-performer-nIBTod4cJI/Steps/1236912)
exposes current, previous, and next coordinates, paths, midsets, counts, and step size. Its value is
not merely replacing paper coordinates; it can make transition information independently
inspectable.

## Rehearsal-process library

### Neutral combined cycle

The sources collectively suggest this research model:

> Prepare → Acquire → Confirm → Describe → Calculate → AC/DC → Rehearse → Check → Adjust → Reset →
> Repeat → Link → Integrate → Clean

This is an index of required functions, not yet a command sequence.

### Kent State check-and-adjust cycle

[Kent State's marching
handbook](https://www-s3-live.kent.edu/s3fs-root/s3fs-public/file/2018%20KSUMGF%20Handbook%20%26%20Syllabus%20%28final%29.pdf?VersionId=UOZpZcKLn7BhE8yscG5MZ4i1aa8U4uNd)
documents a particularly complete loop:

1. Receive tower instruction.
2. Hear a start/end/action recap.
3. Perform from the preceding set through the following set.
4. `CHECK`: privately assess the endpoint without moving.
5. `ADJUST`: move to the correct coordinate, then check cover and dress.
6. Receive tower feedback.
7. Receive field-level correction.
8. Reset and repeat.

The separation of `CHECK` from `ADJUST` preserves diagnostic evidence: students first notice where
they arrived, then correct it.

### Troyka acquisition and linking ideas

The Troyka clinic material recommends:

- locating a destination before moving to it;
- studying the next set while the current one is adjusted;
- marking several sets before marching where useful;
- calculating step size and rehearsing the opening steps;
- freezing on the final step;
- completing several rapid repetitions before lengthy correction;
- linking each new set back to previous material; and
- layering percussion, stationary music, then marching and playing.

### Other operational patterns

- Murphy High School uses a rolling three-set window, traverses moves in both directions, then adds
  instruments, stationary music, and marching with music.
- Kent State uses “minus one” and “plus one” so a cleaned chunk includes its entrances and exits.
- Kent State distinguishes instructional, rehearsal, and performance modes, plus frozen, cold,
  warm, and hot repetitions.
- Troyka separates drum-major direction, whole-group instruction, staff contributions, and
  individual student-leader help into a feedback hierarchy.
- Fast resetting is itself a rehearsed ensemble skill, not merely an instruction to hurry.

## Cleaning lenses

Rather than correcting every category simultaneously, sources can be sorted into:

- endpoint coordinate;
- interval, cover, and form;
- pathway, cross count, and midset;
- step-size consistency;
- timing and feet;
- travel and upper-body orientation;
- marching technique;
- music-plus-motion integration;
- direction changes and recovery into the next phrase; and
- performance consistency under fatigue or altered starting points.

## Candidate evidence of student ownership

A student may demonstrate ownership by being able to:

- use UDB to prepare a short phrase before rehearsal;
- find and explain a coordinate without being placed by staff;
- identify path, counts, step size, and checkpoints;
- perform the AC/DC check after setting a dot;
- assess an endpoint before adjusting it;
- state the form and guide relationship;
- reset correctly on the first instruction;
- carry a correction into the next repetition;
- link several transitions without losing count or direction-change information;
- add music without abandoning established feet and pathway; and
- help verify a peer without assuming responsibility for that peer's dot.

These are candidates for later refinement into observable Band Camp OS outcomes.

## Motor-learning research that may inform later design

These studies are not marching-specific, so the applications are inferences:

- Observational learning supports motor-skill learning in physical education.
  [Systematic review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9407861/)
  - Possible application: UDB animation, live demonstrations, peer models, and student video review.
- Appropriately designed external-focus cues can support children's motor learning.
  [Systematic review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9409566/)
  - Possible application: “travel through the midpoint” or “smooth the arc” can complement
    technique-specific body cues.
- Blocked repetition may help immediate acquisition, while interleaved or varied practice can help
  retention and transfer depending on the task.
  [Transfer review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11349744/);
  [retention review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11237090/)
  - Possible application: move from repeated single transitions to linked chunks, varied starts,
    cold repetitions, and full-context performance.
