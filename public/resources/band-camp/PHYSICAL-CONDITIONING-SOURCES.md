## What the free sample contains

The 14-page sample is a usable first workout, not merely an advertisement. It includes:

- program structure and safety guidance;
- instructions for teaching and leading a session;
- terminology, field setup, short verbal cues, and exercise modifications;
- **Phase 1, Workout A — Strength**, a nine-minute workout at approximately 80 bpm;
- exercise-reference pages for the tempo squat, heel raise with reach, and hip hike; and
- links to the movement-teaching videos.

Workout A uses three bodyweight movements:

1. Tempo squat — 16 repetitions × 3 sets, 4 counts per repetition
2. Heel raise with reach — 20 repetitions × 3 sets, 2 counts per repetition
3. Hip hikes — 8 repetitions per side × 2 sets per side, 4 counts per repetition

The full-season design described by Forte contains nine workouts across three phases and twelve
weeks: two strength sessions and one cardio session per week. The free sample unlocks only Phase 1,
Workout A.

## Source design that may transfer to the Band Camp OS

Forte uses a useful three-layer teaching system:

1. **Learn it:** leaders study the movement videos before teaching.
2. **Reference it:** an exercise library supplies setup, movement, red flags, corrections, and
   modifications.
3. **Run it:** a concise workout page supplies the order, cues, timing, and dose on the field.

This aligns with the Band Camp ownership model: staff or student leaders must learn and demonstrate
the system before they can run it independently. The material also makes workouts operable without
Wi-Fi once the leaders have been trained.

## Safety and governance constraints

The guide says to follow school policies, use qualified adult supervision, allow students to rest
or scale movements, and stop activity when warning signs appear. It gives special attention to heat,
hydration, turf temperature, shade, timing, and emergency response.

Before incorporating this into camp, define:

- who is qualified and responsible for supervising it;
- which school or district health, heat, hydration, and emergency policies govern it;
- how medical restrictions and movement modifications are communicated privately and acted upon;
- how leaders are trained and assessed before they lead students; and
- how the workout fits the visual curriculum without consuming hidden transition or instructional
  time.

This source is physical-conditioning curriculum evidence, not yet an approved daily scheduling
decision. Forte recommends a roughly 15-minute opening routine three days per week when the warmup
is included; Ashley's exact placement must be derived from camp outcomes and safety constraints.
